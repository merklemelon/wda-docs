The idea

A funnel, not a database

Screening the whole market is cheap; reading every 10-K closely is expensive. WDA is built as a funnel: broad, quantitative coverage over the entire public universe narrows to a candidate set, then progressively more expensive processing is applied to progressively fewer names. Every layer below the raw filing is rebuildable from the raw filing with one command — nothing is a black box.

L0 · RAW Filings, stored verbatim
1.07M indexed · 3,989 pulled
SEC EDGAR submission indexes for every company, narrowed to the forms WDA can actually read (10-K / 10-Q / 8-K and their amendments) — then the documents themselves pulled with SEC-compliant rate limits and archived byte-for-byte in object storage. The immutable source of truth.
L1 · NORMALIZED XBRL facts, point-in-time
~5.8M facts
Every reported number carries both its period_end and the date it was filed_at. Restatements are new rows, never overwrites — so you can always ask “what was knowable on date X.” Coverage spans the full filing universe.
L2 · SECTIONS Narrative, cleanly cut
10-K · 10-Q · 8-K items
The Business, Risk Factors and MD&A from the 10-K; the quarterly MD&A and risk-factor update from each 10-Q; the reportable items and press-release exhibit from each 8-K. Cut by walking the filing's own markup rather than pattern-matching the flattened prose, so a contents row and a genuine heading are told apart by structure, not by length.
L3 · DERIVED Financials + valuation
metrics · ratios · multiples
Revenue, margins, R&D intensity, FCF, current ratio, debt/equity — quarters and restatements resolved server-side. Daily prices (× shares) add valuation: market cap, P/E, P/S, EV/EBIT, FCF yield.
L4a · RETRIEVAL The searchable corpus
3,222 companies · 317,550 passages
Narrative cut into passages and embedded, so a question in plain English finds the companies that wrote the answer down. Indexing a filing costs about a fifteenth of a cent, roughly 265× less than reading one with a frontier model — which is why retrieval reaches names that reasoning never will. Widened from the liquid cohort to the whole rankable universe on 2026-09-23.
L4b · REASONING Distilled signal
35 companies
LLM-extracted risk themes, products, forward-looking statements and dated catalysts — typed rows, grounded in the filing, never invented. Every call is logged to a token-cost ledger and gated by a daily spend breaker.
L5 · SCORING The ranked screen
3,712 snapshots
A quality-at-a-reasonable-price composite — margins, growth, balance sheet and valuation multiples, percentile-ranked and point-in-time. Each name rests on a stored, dated factor snapshot (below) so a whole-market ranking is a read, not a recomputation. The funnel's output: the names worth a closer look, in order.

Width ≈ how refined each layer is, not headcount — L5 ranks every name that has a snapshot (3,712 today, all priced); retrieval (L4a) is cheap enough to spread wide, while deep extraction (L4b) stays reserved for the names that earn it.

What it does to a filing

Pulled, normalized, reasoned, extracted

Four verbs, four kinds of output. The first two are deterministic plumbing; the last two are where the research leverage is.

Pulled & normalized — deterministic

The numbers, made comparable

XBRL is standardized in theory and idiosyncratic in practice — NVIDIA tags recent revenue as Revenues while older periods use a different concept; Apple does the opposite. WDA coalesces vendor tags into canonical metrics per period, so a cross-company query just works.

  • Base: revenue, COGS, gross profit, R&D, SG&A, operating & net income, EPS, shares, operating cash flow, capex, balance-sheet items.
  • Ratios: gross / operating / net margin, R&D intensity, FCF & FCF margin, current ratio, debt/equity.
  • Returns on capital: ROE, ROA and ROCE (EBIT / capital employed — pre-tax, because an after-tax figure would need a tax rate reconstructed from other lines). A debt-free balance sheet still gets a ROCE: an untagged debt line means none, not unknown.
  • Valuation: market cap, P/E, P/S, EV/EBIT, FCF yield — from daily closes × shares.
  • Grain: quarterly or annual, most-recent-first, point-in-time correct.
Extracted & reasoned — LLM

The narrative, made structured

A frontier model reads the fiscal year’s narrative — the 10-K’s Items 1, 1A and 7, then each 10-Q’s MD&A (Part I Item 2) and risk-factor update (Part II Item 1A) — and returns typed, filing-grounded rows: never invented, deduplicated, ordered by materiality. Each extraction is logged to a token-cost ledger and gated by a daily spend breaker.

  • risk_themes — material risks, with a severity hint and an is_new flag vs. the prior 10-K; each 10-Q adds a quarterly update (an empty one means the filer reported no material changes).
  • products — the products/services described, with lifecycle stage.
  • forward_statements — guidance, strategy, capex & capital-allocation signals from MD&A.
  • events — dated or binary catalysts (maturities, launches, milestones) from MD&A, plus each 8-K current report and its press release: results and guidance, deals, financings, officer changes, dividends — within hours of filing.
The value-investor edge

Point-in-time, by construction

No lookahead bias baked in

Every fact stores period_end and filed_at, and restatements are appended as new rows rather than replacing the original. A derived read accepts an as_of date and returns only what was actually filed by then — the world as the market saw it, not as it was later corrected.

period_end 2024-12-31 → filed_at 2025-02-14 → as_of 2025-01-31 → not yet visible

Why it matters: back-testing a thesis or a screen on restated, forward-filled fundamentals quietly launders in information nobody had. Here, a query dated to any past day is honest about what was knowable.

Inside the screen

How the ranking is actually computed

The screen is a quality-at-a-reasonable-price composite — seven fundamental factors, five valuation multiples, a price-momentum factor and a liquidity read (both off by default), each turned into a percentile rank within the cohort, then blended by weight. No black box: a transparent, rebuildable recipe you can re-weight per query.

These are the default lens — list_factors returns the exact set, and you can override the weights per query (e.g. crank the valuation bucket for a deep-value tilt, zero out growth, or add momentum: the trailing 12-month return on the split-adjusted close, skipping the last month — a well-replicated premium that diversifies value rather than contradicting it, so it ships at weight 0 and you dial it in).

  1. Measure. Every company carries a stored snapshot of its twelve factors: the quality ratios from its latest complete fiscal year are refreshed the moment a new filing lands, and the multiples (latest close × diluted shares) are revalued every trading night after prices sync. A screen reads those rows — thousands of names in milliseconds — while a dated as_of question still recomputes from the facts as they stood, for a small named set.
  2. Rank each factor. Convert every factor to a percentile within the cohort (0–1), direction-adjusted so higher always means better — the "low is better" factors (P/E, EV/EBIT, debt/equity…) get inverted. Percentile ranks, not z-scores, so one outlier (Micron's 128× P/E) can't distort the scale.
  3. Blend. The composite is the weighted average of the factor percentiles a company actually has — weights renormalized over just those, so a missing factor neither helps nor hurts. Coverage (how many of the twelve had data) is returned alongside, so you can discount a thin score.
  4. Rank & return. Sort by composite, best first. get_score then opens one name's per-factor breakdown and its rank within the cohort.

What is a score relative to? The cohort you choose.

Because each score is a percentile within the cohort, the cohort defines what it means — "top-decile FCF yield" means top-decile of whatever set was scored. Rank against the whole market and it's market-relative; rank against a handful of peers and it's peer-relative. A single company isn't a screen (a percentile needs peers) — for one name use get_score, which reports its rank within the cohort.

So the field you rank over is a real choice — the whole universe, a saved watchlist, or everything matching a live rule. That's what cohorts are, next.

Between the layers

Refresh

Two clocks, one row

  • Fundamentals refresh the moment a company's new facts are ingested — the facts sync chains a snapshot rebuild for that one name.
  • Multiples are re-derived for the whole universe every trading night: prices at 18:00 ET, revaluation at 19:30, theses re-checked at 20:00.
  • A weekly full price refresh (Saturday) re-pulls five years so splits and dividends stay adjusted.
Freshness

Dated, never silently stale

  • Every screen carries a basis block — the newest price_date and valued_at across the snapshot table. Read it as the best case: each company is priced at its own latest close, so a name whose bar was missing that evening sits a day behind the header date.
  • Coverage says how many of the fourteen factors the name actually had — a one-factor name can top a ranking on one ratio, so the guide's advice is to prefer coverage ≥ 6 or say so.
  • Fundamentals are dated per company too: fundamentals_at is each filer’s own latest fiscal year, so a March and a December year-end are compared as filed. That is inherent to any cross-sectional screen.
  • Ask for a date (as_of) and WDA recomputes from the facts instead, for a cohort of at most 50 names — one common date, a backtest rather than a screen.
Why it matters

The funnel's fast path

  • Before snapshots, a whole-market screen scanned millions of facts per question and could take minutes; now it reads 3,712 rows.
  • The snapshot is derived: it is rebuilt from L1 and prices at any time, so it can never be the only copy of a number.
  • New factors join by adding a column to the snapshot, not a new query path — momentum and liquidity arrived that way.

Cohorts

Choose who gets ranked

A cohort is the set the screen ranks over. Since a score is a percentile within that set, choosing the cohort is choosing what the ranking means — the whole market, a peer group, a saved watchlist, or everything matching a live rule. Four ways to say it, all re-weightable and point-in-time.

A rule filters the universe’s factor rows, then ranks what’s left. It re-evaluates every run, so it stays current — save it with create_cohort(rule=…) to reuse by name.

The rule vocabulary

A rule is a small, safe predicate — no code, no free-text — that the model builds from your words (get_rule_fields lists it):

fields — any scoring factor (fcf_yield, ev_ebit, margins, growth, momentum_12_1, adv_63…) + sector (SIC) + gics_sector (the 11 GICS sectors) operators — eq · ne · lt · lte · gt · gte · in · not_in match — all (AND) / any (OR)

Fields are a fixed whitelist, so a rule can never reach arbitrary data. Example — semis with pricing power: [["sector","in",["Semiconductors & Related Devices"]], ["gross_margin","gt",50], ["revenue_growth","gt",10]]. Or think in GICS, the way most planners do: [["gics_sector","eq","Information Technology"], ["fcf_yield","gt",0]]. An investable cohort is one clause: [["adv_63","gte",5000000]] — the screenable universe is not the tradeable one. list_sectors shows the sector values actually present, with counts — the GICS label is a best-effort crosswalk from each company’s SIC code (4-digit overrides, then the 2-digit major group), not an official index assignment.

Forward analysis

What does the price already assume?

Screens rank the present. A high multiple is only understandable through the future it prices in — so WDA runs the DCF backwards: given today’s price, the latest free cash flow, the share count and net debt, it solves for the constant growth rate over a ten-year horizon that would justify the price. That number is the market’s implied expectation, and it is what a thesis argues with.

The reading — band first

A range, never a point

The answer moves with the discount rate, so the tool leads with a sensitivity band — WACC 8 / 10 / 12% × terminal growth 2 / 3% — and only then the headline at your assumptions (defaults 10% · 10 years · 2.5%). Every number is tagged with where it came from: market, fact, market_implied or user_assumption.

price × shares+net debt=what FCF must grow into
Three bases + a flag

Latest · mid-cycle · peak

A cyclical at a trough looks absurdly expensive on last year’s FCF and cheap on its best year. So the growth is solved off three bases — the reported FCF, revenue × the median margin of the last five fiscal years, and revenue × the best margin — and the name is flagged cyclical when they disagree by more than half. The three bracket the truth instead of pretending to know it.

Revenue mode

When FCF is negative

A cash-burning name has no positive base to grow. Pass a target FCF margin (or let the tool use the company’s own best historical margin — never a fabricated default) and it answers the implied revenue growth instead, labelled optimistic because it assumes the margin from year one.

Per share

Buybacks are a translation

Buybacks redistribute cash flow; they don’t create it. The tool reports the net buyback yield (from the diluted share-count trend) and translates total-FCF growth into per-share growth — and withholds it when the share count moved like a split, not a buyback.

Grounded in the narrative, honest about limits

The result arrives with the L4 forward statements, products and events for the name, so the implied growth can be argued against what management actually says it is building. Where the math has no answer — a bank, where FCF is ill-defined; a name with no positive base and no target margin — the tool says so with a status rather than a number.

The assumptions are yours, and they are explicit. Change the WACC and the answer changes; that is the point, not a flaw.

The thesis

An investment case the data can argue with

Most theses live in a document nobody revisits. Here a thesis is a structured, falsifiable object: a stance and a horizon, the claims that must hold, the tripwires that must not occur — each bound to a check over the stack — and WDA re-checks all of it against new filings and prices, every night, indefinitely.

Ask

“Open a medium-conviction, three-year long on Qualcomm: cheap on cash flow, durable margins, and the market’s under-asking. Kill it if gross margin breaks 50 for two quarters, or a new in-house-modem risk shows up at a customer.”

→ create_thesis(…conditions=[fcf_yield > 6% · gross_margin > 50 for 2 quarters · no new modem risk · implied growth < 20%/yr], confirm=false)
previewevery check resolved live before anything is saved — you see the baseline health, then confirm
intactFCF yield 6.4% ✓ · gross margin 55% ✓ · no new modem risk ✓ · implied 5%/yr ✓

Months later, evaluate_thesis answers the only question that matters: which load-bearing condition moved, and when? — “core tripwire tripped on the Oct-30 10-Q: a new Item 1A risk matched ‘in-house modem’ (0.71); gross margin 51%, one of two hysteresis quarters breached.”

The vocabulary

Five kinds of check

  • financial_metric — any canonical metric, annual or quarterly, with hysteresis in periods
  • valuation_metric — P/E, P/S, P/B, EV/EBIT, FCF yield off the dated close
  • factor_percentile — rank on a screen factor within a cohort
  • risk_present — a semantic match in Item 1A themes, optionally only new ones
  • implied_expectations — what the price assumes, from the reverse-DCF
  • price_metric — the absolute close, realised volatility, distance from the 52-week high, or worst drawdown. Risk framing, so “price hasn’t broken below $X” becomes a monitored tripwire. No moving averages, support levels or breakout signals exist, by design

A condition with no check is soft: kept and shown, never scored. Judgment still owns the call.

Units, the one thing that bites. Margins and returns are percentages (roce >= 15 is 15%). FCF yield and every price fraction are fractions (max_drawdown > -0.4 is 40% down). The preview resolves every condition live before anything is saved, which is where a wrong threshold shows itself.

Worked example

Three conditions from a live thesis

price“Has not yet fallen below $100” — on a watch, a reason you are still out. Breaking it means the reason dissolved, and the report names the day.
drawdown“No fall worse than 40% in a year.” Live, this read −39.4% and fired as a near-tripwire — a business screening cheap, net-cash and stable had put holders through a 39% peak-to-trough fall that nothing in the fundamentals mentioned.
roce“Above 15% for two years” — an ordinary financial_metric check, no new type needed, with hysteresis so one soft year is not a verdict.

Distance-from-high and drawdown are separate on purpose: a name that fell 60% and fully recovered reads 0.0 on the first and −0.60 on the second. One asks where the price is, the other what it has done to people who held it.

The verdict

Honest by construction

  • core broken ⇒ thesis broken; core weakening or supporting broken ⇒ weakening; else intact
  • missing data ⇒ unknown — never a silent pass
  • near-tripwires: supported conditions within 10% of their bar
  • a pinned valuation anchor beside what the price implies now — has the market moved the bar?
  • as_of backtests the whole report to any past date; the history shows what changed and when

Place it

The macro backdrop

  • list_macro_series — what is on record, how fresh, how deep
  • get_macro_series — one series, newest first, point-in-time

Eight series, ten years each: the 10- and 2-year Treasury, the fed funds rate, the 10y–2y spread, CPI, unemployment, WTI crude and Henry Hub gas. Refreshed nightly at 17:30 ET, before the price sync, so the evening’s reads see the same day’s rates and the same day’s closes.

Why this is not a breach of the primary-sources rule. These are statistical agencies publishing their own measurements — the same category as a company publishing its own filing. What stays out is anyone’s opinion about what the numbers mean.

Guardrail

Safe by default

Anything that spends money or writes data is a two-step preview → confirm — one confirm per intent, not per step — and enrichment is capped by a daily budget breaker. You see the cost before it runs, and if a step fails you see why: get_pipeline_status and get_ingest_status carry the error text.

Coverage today

Two tiers, not one

Retrieval and reasoning were separated once the cost gap between them became clear. 3,222 companies are narrative-searchable — 317,550 embedded passages — which is every rankable company that files a 10-K. The corpus widened from the liquid cohort to the whole screenable universe on 2026-09-23, so the invariant is now the simple one: anything you can rank, you can also read. 35 carry the full extraction stack on top of that: typed risk themes, products, guidance and dated catalysts. The value screen ranks 3,712 names on fundamentals, all priced to the previous close. Extraction is applied where it earns its keep, not sprayed across the universe — and any company can be promoted into it with a single request.

In one breath

Why it’s useful to a value investor

  1. Comparable fundamentals, instantly. Ask for any metric across any set of names and get quarter-aligned, restatement-resolved series — the tedious part of primary research, gone.
  2. The narrative as data. Risk factors, products, guidance and catalysts become queryable rows — and the year-over-year change in disclosed risk is surfaced explicitly.
  3. Thematic screening by meaning. “Who is exposed to foundry concentration?” or “who is guiding higher capex?” — answered across the corpus, not one filing at a time.
  4. Value and rank, in one place. Real multiples — P/E, FCF yield, EV/EBIT — and a quality-at-a-reasonable-price composite that ranks the fact-having universe, best first.
  5. Rank on your own terms. Screen the whole market, a saved watchlist, or everything matching a live rule — the cohort you choose sets what every percentile is relative to.
  6. What the price assumes. A reverse-DCF restates any multiple as the growth path it requires — a range, with three bases and explicit assumptions — so a thesis argues with the market’s number, not a hunch.
  7. A thesis that answers back. Claims and tripwires bound to the data, re-checked every night — so the question “is my case still true?” has an answer with a date on it.
  8. Honest history. Point-in-time facts and prices mean a thesis or a screen tested on the past is tested on what was actually knowable — no lookahead.
  9. You just ask. Natural language in, structured research out — the model drives forty tools so you don’t have to.
